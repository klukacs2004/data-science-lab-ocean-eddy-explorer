1. Physical motivation

The Nencioli method identifies eddy centers from the geometry of horizontal velocity vectors. A rotating flow usually has a low-speed core, opposite velocity components on opposite sides of that core, and coherent circulation around it. Four constraints test these properties in sequence.

Reference: Nencioli, Dong, Dickey, Washburn and McWilliams (2010), A Vector Geometry–Based Eddy Detection Algorithm and Its Application to a High-Resolution Numerical Model Product and High-Frequency Radar Surface Velocities in the Southern California Bight. Center and boundary detection are described in section 3a, pp. 567–572; streamfunction computation is described in the appendix, p. 578.

Unlike the Chelton explorer, this method does not select regions by SSH thresholds, a minimum SSH amplitude, or a pixel-count criterion. It operates on velocity. SLA appears as map context, not as an acceptance condition.

The detection explorer investigates one day at a time; the tracking explorer associates these daily results as described in section 8. Detecting a center and estimating its boundary are separate operations: a center can pass all four constraints but have no accepted boundary.

2. Velocity field and coordinates

The input consists of eastward velocity \(u\) and northward velocity \(v\), in metres per second, on the same rectilinear grid. Arrays are indexed \((i,j)\), with latitude along rows and longitude along columns. Increasing \(i\) moves north; increasing \(j\) moves east.

\[ S_{i,j}=\sqrt{u_{i,j}^{\,2}+v_{i,j}^{\,2}}. \]

Here \(S\) is horizontal speed. Both coordinate arrays must be finite and strictly increasing, and both velocity arrays must have matching dimensions. Land and unavailable observations remain missing values; they are not filled with artificial zero velocities.

The explorer uses the daily geostrophic velocity fields supplied in the OSU blended-altimetry dataset on a 0.25° grid. It does not apply the Chelton page's Gaussian background removal to these velocities or add a mean current field. Detections therefore characterize the supplied flow rather than establishing the circulation of the full absolute current.

Two parameters control center detection: \(a\), the velocity-growth search distance and circulation scale, and \(b\), the speed-minimum search half-width. They are measured in grid cells, not kilometres. In this implementation \(a\geq 2\) and \(b\geq 1\).

3. Detecting eddy centers

The algorithm first selects adjacent east–west pairs, then independently tests each member as a candidate. A candidate advances only when the previous constraint passes. A refined minimum already recorded as a center is marked as a duplicate rather than counted as another eddy.

3.1 East–west velocity reversal and growth

For adjacent points \((i,j)\) and \((i,j+1)\), the northward velocity component must have opposite signs. Its magnitude must be larger at points \(a\) cells farther west and east, with the respective signs preserved.

\[ \begin{aligned} v_W &=v_{i,j}, & v_E &=v_{i,j+1},\\ v_{W,o} &=v_{i,j-a}, & v_{E,o} &=v_{i,j+1+a}. \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{gathered} v_Wv_E\lt 0,\\ v_{W,o}v_W\gt 0,\qquad v_{E,o}v_E\gt 0,\\ |v_{W,o}|\gt |v_W|,\qquad |v_{E,o}|\gt |v_E|. \end{gathered} \]

Strict sign reversal means a zero component does not form a qualifying opposite-sign pair. All sampled values must be finite and lie within the grid. These conditions identify possible rotation, not a center by themselves: shear and meanders can also produce sign changes.

3.2 North–south velocity reversal and growth

Each member \((i,k)\), where \(k\in\{j,j+1\}\), is tested separately. The eastward velocity at \(a\) cells south and north must reverse sign, imply the same eddy rotation as the first constraint, and have greater magnitude than the eastward component at the original candidate.

\[ \begin{gathered} u_S=u_{i-a,k},\qquad u_C=u_{i,k},\qquad u_N=u_{i+a,k},\\ u_Su_N\lt 0,\qquad |u_S|\gt |u_C|,\qquad |u_N|\gt |u_C|. \end{gathered} \]
Rotation about the center West \(v_W\) East \(v_E\) South \(u_S\) North \(u_N\)
Clockwise Positive Negative Negative Positive
Counterclockwise Negative Positive Positive Negative

These clockwise/counterclockwise sign patterns follow the eastward and northward coordinate directions. Cyclonic versus anticyclonic terminology additionally depends on hemisphere. In the Northern Hemisphere, counterclockwise circulation is cyclonic.

3.3 A stable local speed minimum

The first two constraints nominate search locations. The third refines each location to a local minimum of speed. Let \(W_b(p)\) be the square window extending \(b\) cells in both coordinate directions from point \(p\).

\[ m_1=\underset{q\in W_b(p)}{\operatorname{arg\,min}}\,S(q), \qquad m_2=\underset{q\in W_b(m_1)}{\operatorname{arg\,min}}\,S(q). \]

The refined point is retained only if \(m_2=m_1\). Thus the minimum must remain a minimum when a second window is centered on it. For \(b=3\), each search uses a 7 × 7 window; for \(b=2\), it uses a 5 × 5 window. Both windows must fit inside the grid and contain only finite speeds. Ties follow the array's deterministic first-minimum ordering.

A low-speed point alone is not sufficient: a meander or a shear region can also contain a stable minimum. The fourth constraint tests circulation around that minimum.

3.4 Consistent circulation around the minimum

A square perimeter is constructed at \(a-1\) cells from the refined minimum along each axis. Its vectors are visited counterclockwise, starting at the southwest corner. The perimeter has \(8(a-1)\) samples, with each corner visited once.

For velocity direction \(\theta_n\), the angular change between successive vectors, including the closing pair, is

\[ \theta_n=\operatorname{atan2}(v_n,u_n),\qquad \delta_n=\operatorname{Arg}\!\left[ \exp\!\left(\mathrm{i}(\theta_{n+1}-\theta_n)\right) \right]. \]

The directions must progress without reversing, and consecutive vectors must lie in the same quadrant or the next quadrant counterclockwise. The implementation also verifies one complete turn:

\[ \delta_n\geq-\varepsilon,\qquad (Q_{n+1}-Q_n)\bmod 4\in\{0,1\},\qquad \sum_n\delta_n\simeq 2\pi, \]

where \(Q_n\) is the quadrant of the vector and \(\varepsilon\) is a small numerical tolerance. Missing vectors and zero-speed vectors fail because their direction cannot establish a complete circulation.

Perimeter traversal is not the eddy's rotation direction. With a counterclockwise traversal, vector directions progress counterclockwise for both clockwise and counterclockwise vortices. Reversing the eddy reverses every velocity vector; it does not reverse this spatial progression. The first two constraints determine the eddy's clockwise or counterclockwise label.

4. Estimating eddy boundaries

After a center passes the four constraints, a separate local calculation estimates its boundary. This stage uses velocity-derived streamfunction contours, not the SSH threshold regions used by the Chelton method.

4.1 Local streamfunction

Following the paper's sign convention, the streamfunction differential is

\[ \mathrm{d}\psi=-v\,\mathrm{d}x+u\,\mathrm{d}y. \]

The southwest corner of the local region is assigned zero streamfunction. Two integration paths reach each point: east then north, or north then east. Both paths are evaluated with trapezoidal sums and averaged, as in appendix equations A1–A4.

\[ \begin{aligned} \psi_{xy}(x,y) &=-\int_{x_0}^{x}v(\xi,y_0)\,\mathrm{d}\xi +\int_{y_0}^{y}u(x,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta,\\ \psi_{yx}(x,y) &=\int_{y_0}^{y}u(x_0,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta -\int_{x_0}^{x}v(\xi,y)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\\ \psi(x,y)&=\tfrac12\left(\psi_{xy}+\psi_{yx}\right). \end{aligned} \]

Real velocity fields are not perfectly divergence-free, so the paths can give different values. Averaging reduces path dependence; it does not eliminate it or guarantee that every contour is an exact streamline.

On the geographic grid, physical spacings are approximated by

\[ \Delta x\simeq R_\oplus\cos\phi\,\Delta\lambda, \qquad \Delta y\simeq R_\oplus\,\Delta\phi, \]

with angular increments in radians and \(R_\oplus=6{,}371{,}000\) m. The resulting streamfunction has units of m²/s. A contour's fractional grid coordinates are interpolated to latitude and longitude for maps and distance calculations.

4.2 The outermost contour with radial speed growth

The implementation samples 100 streamfunction levels between the local minimum and maximum. A candidate contour must be closed and contain the detected center. It must also pass a radial speed-growth test at its northernmost, southernmost, easternmost, and westernmost points.

For each extremum \(p\) and center \(c\), the current numerical stencil is

\[ \widehat{e}_r=\frac{p-c}{\lVert p-c\rVert},\qquad p_{\mathrm{in}}=p-\tfrac12\widehat{e}_r,\qquad p_{\mathrm{out}}=p+\tfrac12\widehat{e}_r, \]
\[ S(p_{\mathrm{out}})\gt S(p_{\mathrm{in}}) \quad\text{at all four extrema}. \]

Positions in this stencil are fractional grid indices; speed is bilinearly interpolated. An extremum within one grid cell of the center, an out-of-region sample, or a non-finite sample fails the test. Among the passing contours, the largest fractional polygon area selects the outermost boundary. This area ranks contours; it is not reported as a physical eddy area.

The four-extremum growth condition comes from the paper. The 100 contour levels, half-cell sampling stencil, and small-contour guard are explicit numerical choices in this implementation. In particular, radial steps are measured in grid-index space, whose physical scales differ between longitude and latitude.

4.3 Expanding and reducing the local region

The initial region extends \(2a\) cells from the center in each direction, giving a \((4a+1)\times(4a+1)\) array. If an accepted contour lies less than 1.5 cells from a local edge, the half-width is increased by \(a\) and the streamfunction and boundary are recomputed.

If the region reaches the data boundary or includes land or missing velocities, its half-width is reduced one cell at a time until a finite region fits. Once limited in this way it is not enlarged again. The current implementation stops without a boundary if no valid region with half-width at least \(a\) can be used.

The diagnostics retain land/missing-data and domain-edge limitation flags. A flag means that the local integration region was constrained; it does not necessarily mean that the final contour itself was cut by an edge.

4.4 Circular estimates and the reported radius

If no closed streamfunction contour surrounds the center, the paper allows an estimated circular boundary of radius \(a-1\) grid cells. That fallback is retained and labelled Estimated circle. A circle in grid-index coordinates need not be a physical circle on a latitude–longitude grid.

This fallback is not used when closed contours exist but all fail the growth test. That outcome is recorded as a detected center without an accepted boundary.

For an accepted contour with \(N\) distinct sampled points \(p_n\), the reported radius is the mean great-circle distance from the center:

\[ R_{\mathrm{eddy}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}d_{\mathrm{gc}}(c,p_n). \]

Distances are calculated on a sphere using the haversine formula, in kilometres, and the duplicated closing point is excluded from the mean. This is not the Chelton explorer's area-equivalent radius, a diameter, or an independently estimated radius of maximum speed.

5. Parameter sensitivity and physical scale

Parameter Role Interpretation
\(a\) Velocity-growth offsets and the \(a-1\) circulation perimeter A larger value tests coherence over a larger neighborhood; it can miss smaller or distorted features.
\(b\) Half-width of both speed-minimum searches Controls how far a candidate can move to a stable minimum. Several searches can reach the same center.
Contour levels Numerical boundary sampling; default 100 Separate from the method's two tuning parameters. Small contours can be missed or change size with coarse sampling.

The paper used \(a=4,b=3\) for its 1 km model example. Those values are not a universal setting. The OSU grid spacing is 0.25°: approximately 27.8 km north–south, with a smaller, latitude-dependent east–west spacing. The same integer parameters therefore test much larger physical scales.

The explorer's parameter sweep counts centers passing the four constraints. Boundary selection runs when a configuration is inspected. Consequently, a sweep count is not the number of accepted boundaries. More detections alone do not establish a better parameter choice.

For the evaluated 1993-01-01 field, \(a=2,b=2\) yields 16 centers and 6 accepted boundaries; \(a=4,b=3\) yields no centers. These are results on this particular regional field, not the paper's published counts or evidence that \(a=2,b=2\) is optimal on other dates.

6. Interpreting candidate diagnostics

The candidate table starts after criterion 1: every qualifying east–west pair produces two independent searches. Rejected searches are assigned their first failed check. Subsequent checks are marked Not evaluated, rather than failed.

Decision Meaning
North–south velocity Signs, growth, or rotation agreement fail at the second constraint.
Stable speed minimum The minimum searches disagree, or a required window is unavailable or contains missing data.
Perimeter outside grid The circulation square cannot be evaluated within the data domain.
Closed circulation The perimeter contains missing/zero vectors, reverses angular progression, skips a quadrant, or fails the full-turn check.
Duplicate center The search reaches an already recorded center. It is not a separate eddy or another center failure.
Insufficient ocean region No usable local region is available for boundary estimation.
Boundary radial growth Closed contours exist, but none passes the implemented growth test.
Boundary accepted A center has a passing streamfunction contour or a labelled circular estimate.

Pair counts, search counts, unique-center counts, and accepted-boundary counts are different quantities. Candidate IDs are specific to the selected date and parameter configuration, and duplicates are reported separately.

The inspector shows the original candidate, refined minimum, velocity sections, search neighborhoods, angular progression, and available boundary-growth evidence. Some plots illustrate geometry for checks that were not reached; the outcome table records the actual evaluation. The largest rejected contour may be shown as an example, not as an accepted boundary.

7. Comparison with the paper and limits

Aspect What is preserved What to account for here
Center detection The four sequential velocity-geometry constraints, two minimum searches, and \(a-1\) perimeter. Strict missing-data handling, a numerical full-turn check, and deterministic minimum tie handling are explicit implementation details.
Boundary estimation Two-path streamfunction integration, growth at four extrema, adaptive local regions, and the documented circular fallback. Contour sampling, polygon ranking, the half-cell stencil, and geographic grid metrics influence the estimated boundary.
Input data Detection uses horizontal velocity, as required by the method. This regional daily geostrophic dataset differs from the paper's model and HF-radar examples. Resolution and the supplied velocity field determine which features can be resolved.
Tracking Daily detection is followed by same-polarity center association in the Tracking Explorer (section 8). This project's distance-based greedy tracking and configurable calendar-span filtering are adaptations. Single-day detection counts remain snapshot counts, not tracked-catalogue counts.
Validation Diagnostics expose the criteria and measurements used for each decision. Geometric consistency is not an independent truth label. Success and excess-of-detection rates require an independently annotated eddy field.

Features with radii of only one or two grid cells remain poorly resolved. Parameter sensitivity, contour-resolution checks, and visual inspection help assess numerical behavior, but they do not by themselves establish detection accuracy, dynamical coherence, or long-term persistence.

8. Tracking eddies across days

The Nencioli Tracking Explorer runs daily detection and then associates accepted detections through time. This section describes track_nencioli() in this project: distance-based searches and greedy conflict resolution are practical adaptations, not an exact reproduction of the paper's grid-window tracking implementation.

8.1 Daily detections and center coordinates

For each calendar day in the selected interval, the explorer loads the supplied eastward and northward velocity fields on an exactly aligned grid, applies the selected \(a,b\) configuration, and converts accepted results with standardize_nencioli(). Detection is independent on each day; a previous track does not influence the next day's center search. Only results with an accepted boundary, including accepted circular estimates, enter tracking.

Each standardized detection carries a date, polarity, contour, and size. Tracking distances and trajectory coordinates prefer the native velocity-based grid center. If either native coordinate is unavailable, both coordinates fall back to the standardized spherical mean of contour vertices. The tracking page reports the standardized area-equivalent radius \(r=\sqrt{A/\pi}\), where \(A\) is the spherical contour area; this differs from the detection explorer's mean center-to-boundary radius in section 4.4.

Unavailable source dates are inserted as empty detection lists and listed on the results page. An available day with no accepted detections is also empty for association. Processing errors stop the run with a form error; they are not silently converted into missing observations. The explorer rejects coordinate-grid changes within the selected interval.

8.2 Great-circle search distance

The tracker uses the haversine distance between the last observed center and a candidate center. With latitudes \(\phi_1,\phi_2\), wrapped longitude difference \(\Delta\lambda\in[-\pi,\pi)\), and angles in radians:

\[ h=\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\phi_2-\phi_1}{2}\right) +\cos\phi_1\cos\phi_2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Delta\lambda}{2}\right), \qquad d=2R\arcsin\sqrt{\operatorname{clip}(h,0,1)}, \quad R=6371\ \mathrm{km}. \]

Candidate polarity must equal the track's last observed polarity exactly. Unknown polarity therefore matches only unknown polarity here. No contour IoU, radius similarity, amplitude criterion, or motion prediction is used.

8.3 Two-pass association and conflict resolution

  1. Adjacent-day pass. For each active track last observed yesterday, collect today's same-polarity candidates with \(d\leq r_{\mathrm{daily}}\), default 30 km.
  2. Greedy assignment. Sort all candidates in this pass by increasing distance. Accept a pair only if neither the track nor today's detection has already been assigned. Equal-distance ties use track ID, then the detection's position in today's input list.
  3. One-day-gap pass. Using the remaining unassigned detections, repeat for tracks last observed two calendar days ago with \(d\leq r_{\mathrm{gap}}\), default 45 km. Adjacent-day assignments have priority even if a gap candidate would be closer. The gap threshold is the total two-day displacement, not a per-day radius.
  4. Close or start tracks. After both passes, close tracks whose latest observation is more than one day old. Today's remaining detections each start a new track with an incrementing run-local ID.

A track may bridge one missing detection day at a time, and may do so repeatedly if observations resume between gaps. Two consecutive missing days close it. No observation is inserted during a gap, and closed tracks are not reopened. Each detection belongs to at most one track; tracks do not split or merge. The greedy procedure does not optimize complete trajectories jointly.

The Tracking Explorer uses this greedy, two-pass procedure with one-day recovery enabled. Its form exposes the daily and gap radii, rather than an assignment-method selector or a switch to disable gaps. These are the settings described here, even though the underlying tracking function also supports other association configurations.

8.5 Reading trajectories and repeated centers

The map uses solid connections for adjacent days and separated dash segments across a missing detection day. Only markers represent observations. The selected-track time series shows each observed date's great-circle distance from that track's first assigned center, not cumulative path length or speed; missing dates are not interpolated.

Grid points show dataset centers with non-NaN SLA on the first available day in the run. This is a display mask, not a tracking acceptance criterion or a guarantee of valid velocity on every day. Hover counts report how many observations within the same track share the exact center coordinates.

Six identical coordinates mean that the detector assigned the same grid location on six dates, not that the physical eddy was stationary. The repeated-center summary counts accepted tracks with at least two observations using a single center throughout, and identical-center observation pairs on consecutive calendar days (excluding gaps). Frequent repeats can motivate checking grid resolution, velocity fields, and parameter sensitivity; these counts alone do not diagnose a detector error.

Track endpoints are limited by the selected interval and data coverage. Associations do not establish material coherence or an independently validated eddy identity. Larger radii can connect distinct nearby features; smaller radii and stricter lifetime filtering can fragment or remove real trajectories. Processing is synchronous and capped at 30 days in this explorer.
