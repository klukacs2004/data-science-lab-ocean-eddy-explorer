1. Method and scope

The SSH-based method of Chelton et al. identifies compact regions enclosed by sea-surface-height contours. It avoids estimating eddy boundaries from velocity derivatives, which can amplify noise. Candidate regions must pass geometric and amplitude conditions before they are retained.

Reference: Chelton, Schlax and Samelson (2011), Global observations of nonlinear mesoscale eddies, Progress in Oceanography 91, 167–216. Appendix B.2 defines identification, B.3 amplitude and scales, B.4 tracking, and Appendix C discusses boundary and amplitude bias.

The detection explorer implements single-map identification and selected measurements from Appendix B.2–B.3. The separate tracking explorer links these daily detections using the regional adaptation in section 6. Neither reproduces the paper's full tracked catalogue, speed-based scale, or nonlinearity analysis. An accepted patch is a snapshot detection; persistence is investigated separately through temporal association.

The input here is daily OSU blended altimetry over the U.S. West Coast on a 0.25° grid. The paper analyzed global 0.25° AVISO Reference Series SSH maps sampled every seven days over 1992–2008. Equal nominal grid spacing does not guarantee equal effective resolution or identical detections.

2. SSH geometry and circulation

Sea-level anomaly is SSH relative to a reference mean:

\[ \eta'(x,y,t)=\eta(x,y,t)-\overline{\eta}(x,y). \]

For an anomaly field in approximate geostrophic balance, the eastward and northward velocity anomalies satisfy

\[ u'_g=-\frac{g}{f}\frac{\partial\eta'}{\partial y},\qquad v'_g=\frac{g}{f}\frac{\partial\eta'}{\partial x},\qquad f=2\Omega\sin\phi. \]

Consequently, \(\mathbf v'_g\cdot\nabla\eta'=0\): geostrophic velocity anomalies are tangent to SLA contours. This motivates using closed SSH contours as candidate perimeters. The snapshot detector does not need to compute these derivatives or apply an Okubo–Weiss threshold.

A concave-down local SSH maximum indicates anticyclonic circulation in the Northern Hemisphere; a concave-up minimum indicates cyclonic circulation. Clockwise versus counterclockwise rotation reverses in the Southern Hemisphere. The absolute sign of SLA is not the polarity: a local maximum can lie entirely below zero on a negative background.

These equations describe velocity anomalies associated with the chosen field. Total geostrophic currents also depend on the background dynamic sea-surface height. Closed anomaly contours alone do not establish material trapping or long-term dynamical coherence.

3. Identifying candidate regions

Let \(h\) denote the field actually used for detection, in metres. It is the processed anomaly field when background removal is selected, and the input SLA when no additional filtering is selected. Thresholds displayed in centimetres are converted to metres before evaluation.

3.1 Background removal and its physical scale

Appendix B.2 uses spatial high-pass filtering to attenuate coherent large-scale signals, with cutoff wavelengths of 20° longitude by 10° latitude. This is distinct from the smoothing already present in the mapped altimeter product.

The explorer offers a custom Gaussian background subtraction, a Gaussian approximation calibrated to those stated cutoff scales, and no additional filtering. For the Gaussian options, the background and detection field are

\[ G(\xi,\zeta)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_\lambda\sigma_\phi} \exp\!\left[-\frac12\left( \frac{\xi^2}{\sigma_\lambda^2}+\frac{\zeta^2}{\sigma_\phi^2} \right)\right], \]
\[ b=\frac{G*(M\eta')}{G*M},\qquad h=\eta'-b. \]

Here \(M\) is one at valid ocean pixels and zero elsewhere. The numerator uses zero-filled invalid pixels; division normalizes by the valid weight. Missing pixels remain missing, and division is performed only where the denominator is sufficiently large. Land is not treated as a physical zero-SLA observation.

For an ideal Gaussian on an unbounded uniform grid, the axial high-pass response and half-power calibration are

\[ H(k)=1-\exp(-2\pi^2\sigma^2k^2),\qquad |H(1/\lambda_c)|^2=\frac12, \]
\[ \sigma=\frac{\lambda_c}{2\pi} \sqrt{-2\ln\!\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\right)}. \]

Wavelength and Gaussian scale must use consistent coordinate units. On this 0.25° grid, the default custom \(\sigma=(4,4)\) cells corresponds to one degree of Gaussian standard deviation per axis and ideal high-pass half-power wavelengths of about 4.01° × 4.01°. The paper-scale Gaussian option instead uses approximately 19.95 longitude cells and 9.98 latitude cells to match 20° × 10° axial cutoff scales.

The Gaussian option is an approximation. Matching the reported cutoff wavelengths does not verify the paper's original filter kernel. The finite regional domain, coastlines, and mask normalization modify the response from the ideal calculation. The explorer therefore compares filter choices on the same date rather than presenting one as an exact reproduction of the global analysis.

3.2 Polarity and threshold selection

At threshold \(T\), maximum-seeking and minimum-seeking pixels are selected separately from the currently available finite pixels \(V\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \Omega_{\max}(T;V)&=\{p\in V:h(p)\gt T\},\\ \Omega_{\min}(T;V)&=\{p\in V:h(p)\lt T\}. \end{aligned} \]

Each connected component of the appropriate set becomes a candidate. The comparison is strict, so a pixel exactly equal to the threshold is excluded. Both polarities are tested at thresholds of either sign, as in the paper's Fig. B1. Selecting a negative threshold does not switch a maximum search into a minimum search.

At a single threshold, the page shows that level's candidate geometry. The full scan searches for the outermost acceptable regions over many levels. These are different analysis modes, so their detection counts need not agree.

3.3 Connectivity, interior, and perimeter pixels

Two pixels are connected if they share a north, south, east, or west edge; diagonal contact alone is insufficient. For grid point \((i,j)\), define

\[ N_4(i,j)=\{(i-1,j),(i+1,j),(i,j-1),(i,j+1)\}. \]

For a candidate component \(\Omega\), interior pixels have all four nearest neighbors inside the same region. Perimeter pixels are the remaining candidate pixels:

\[ I(\Omega)=\{p\in\Omega:N_4(p)\subseteq\Omega\},\qquad P(\Omega)=\Omega\setminus I(\Omega). \]

The region must also be simply connected: it cannot enclose holes outside the selected region. The same four-neighbor definition determines which pixels can contain qualifying extrema, which pixels enter the perimeter mean, and which accepted pixels are retired during scanning.

A qualifying maximum must be interior and strictly exceed each neighbor; a minimum must be interior and strictly fall below each neighbor:

\[ \begin{aligned} p_{\max}\in I(\Omega):&\quad h(p_{\max})\gt h(q)\quad\forall q\in N_4(p_{\max}),\\ p_{\min}\in I(\Omega):&\quad h(p_{\min})\lt h(q)\quad\forall q\in N_4(p_{\min}). \end{aligned} \]

A peak lying on the perimeter does not qualify. Neither does a flat plateau without a strict four-neighbor extremum. Conversely, several qualifying extrema are allowed within one accepted patch. The paper abandoned automatic splitting because it introduced undesirable changes in tracked eddy properties; this implementation likewise does not split a region solely because it has multiple extrema.

3.4 The paper's five acceptance criteria

Appendix B.2 defines an eddy as a simply connected set of pixels satisfying all five conditions below. The numerical bounds are part of that identification procedure, not evidence by themselves of a nonlinear eddy.

Criterion Requirement Purpose
1 · Threshold Every pixel is above \(T\) for a maximum search or below \(T\) for a minimum search. Defines a connected SSH region of the chosen polarity.
2 · Pixel count \(8\leq |\Omega|\lt 1000\). Excludes undersized and excessively large connected regions.
3 · Interior extremum At least one qualifying local maximum or minimum. Requires a core inside the region rather than a perimeter peak.
4 · Amplitude \(A\geq 0.01\) m. Retains features with amplitude of at least 1 cm.
5 · Compactness \(D_{\max}\lt D_{\mathrm{lim}}\). Limits elongated features and broad outer flanges.

The 8-pixel minimum was motivated by the effective smoothing of the paper's 0.25° AVISO product: an approximately 0.4°-radius circle occupies about eight such pixels. This rationale concerns the input mapping resolution, not the separate 20° × 10° background-removal step. The same nominal pixel count does not impose a constant physical area at all latitudes.

The maximum distance is calculated over every pair of candidate pixel centers using great-circle distances:

\[ D_{\max}=\max_{p,q\in\Omega}d_{\mathrm{gc}}(p,q). \]

The paper uses 400 km poleward of 25° latitude, increasing linearly to 1200 km at the equator. In this explorer, that rule is evaluated at the candidate's mean pixel latitude \(\bar\phi\):

\[ D_{\mathrm{lim}}(\bar\phi)= \begin{cases} 400\ \mathrm{km}, & |\bar\phi|\geq 25^\circ,\\ \left[1200-800\dfrac{|\bar\phi|}{25^\circ}\right]\mathrm{km}, & |\bar\phi|\lt 25^\circ. \end{cases} \]

All current U.S. West Coast candidates use the 400 km limit. Equality fails the distance test, while an amplitude exactly 1 cm passes its lower bound. The implementation computes pair distances in bounded vectorized blocks; it does not replace the all-pairs criterion with a bounding box or an area-based diameter.

Additional regional data guards reject candidates touching the cropped map boundary or adjacent missing pixels, because their closed SSH contour cannot be established from the available field. These are explicit implementation choices beyond the five numbered conditions.

3.5 Full threshold scanning and pixel retirement

To avoid selecting one fixed SSH level, Appendix B.2 scans the two polarities independently. The explorer follows these directions:

The paper describes this as threshold-free identification because a single SSH contour level need not be chosen. The method still has explicit design choices, including the 1 cm step, amplitude floor, pixel-count bounds, and compactness limit.

Search Threshold sequence [cm] Selected values
Maximum / concave down −100, −99, …, +100 \(h\gt T\)
Minimum / concave up +100, +99, …, −100 \(h\lt T\)

Each polarity starts with its own availability mask containing all finite pixels. At each threshold, connected candidates are evaluated. When a region is accepted, only its interior pixels are removed from subsequent searches of that polarity:

\[ V_{n+1}=V_n\setminus \bigcup_{\Omega\in\mathcal E_n}I(\Omega), \]

where \(\mathcal E_n\) is the set of regions accepted at threshold step \(n\). Perimeter pixels remain available; the other polarity's mask is unchanged. This order aims to retain the largest acceptable regions encountered before stricter levels isolate smaller interiors.

The scan uses a 1 cm increment. The paper found that reducing the step could retain broad, flat, irregular outer flanges rather than improving compact eddy boundaries. The 1 cm increment is therefore a deliberate compromise, not a claim that every physical eddy boundary is known to centimetre accuracy.

Candidate counts across a scan include separate connected-region decisions at different thresholds. They are not counts of distinct physical eddies. Each candidate receives a unique scan ID, and accepted results preserve that ID in maps, tables, exports, and inspection.

4. Measuring accepted patches

4.1 Perimeter mean and eddy amplitude

Appendix B.3 estimates the basal height \(h_0\) as the arithmetic mean of SSH over the perimeter pixels, whose values need not be identical on a discretized grid:

\[ h_0=\frac{1}{|P(\Omega)|}\sum_{p\in P(\Omega)}h(p),\qquad h_{\max}=\max_{p\in\Omega}h(p),\qquad h_{\min}=\min_{p\in\Omega}h(p). \]
\[ A= \begin{cases} h_{\max}-h_0, & \text{maximum / concave down},\\ h_0-h_{\min}, & \text{minimum / concave up}. \end{cases} \]

Amplitude is positive for either polarity and is tested against 1 cm. The threshold \(T\) is not substituted for \(h_0\). On a sloping SSH surface, perimeter pixels can differ appreciably from the threshold.

Illustrative feature Threshold Perimeter mean Extreme SSH Amplitude
Maximum on a negative background −13 cm −12 cm −4 cm 8 cm
Minimum on a positive background +13 cm +12 cm +4 cm 8 cm

These are explanatory values, not observations from the dataset. They illustrate why neither the sign of SSH nor the threshold itself defines amplitude or polarity.

4.2 Geographic area, effective radius, and compactness

The explorer sums spherical cell areas to estimate the patch area. With angular cell edges in radians and \(R_\oplus=6371\) km,

\[ a_{ij}=R_\oplus^2(\lambda_{i+1/2}-\lambda_{i-1/2}) \left[\sin\phi_{j+1/2}-\sin\phi_{j-1/2}\right],\qquad \mathcal A=\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega}a_{ij}, \]
\[ L_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal A}{\pi}}. \]

Cell edges are inferred from coordinate midpoints, with the outside edges extrapolated by half a grid spacing. Detection arrays use longitude × latitude ordering; plotting transposes them. The resulting \(L_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is an area-equivalent radius in kilometres.

Great-circle pair distances use the haversine expression

\[ \begin{aligned} z&=\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\phi_2-\phi_1}{2}\right) +\cos\phi_1\cos\phi_2 \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\lambda_2-\lambda_1}{2}\right),\\ d_{\mathrm{gc}}&=2R_\oplus\arcsin\sqrt z. \end{aligned} \]

The maximum of these distances is \(D_{\max}\), a compactness measure. It is generally not \(2L_{\mathrm{eff}}\). An elongated patch can have a modest area-equivalent radius yet fail the maximum-pair-distance criterion.

4.3 The paper's different radius definitions

Appendix B.3 distinguishes several scales. Background-filter Gaussian \(\sigma\), Gaussian eddy-shape scales, area-equivalent radius, and speed-based radius describe different quantities.

Quantity Definition or role in the paper Available here?
\(L_{\mathrm{eff}}\) Radius of a circle with the same area as the accepted outer region. Yes, from geographic pixel areas.
\(L_e\) Gaussian-derived e-folding scale, estimated from the area above \(h_0+A/e\) for maxima or below \(h_0-A/e\) for minima. Not computed.
\(L=L_e/\sqrt2\) Radius of maximum axial geostrophic speed for an ideal axisymmetric Gaussian eddy. Not computed.
\(L_s\) Area-equivalent radius of the SSH contour with maximum circum-average geostrophic speed. Not computed.
\(D_{\max}\) Maximum point-pair distance used to constrain compactness. Yes; reported separately from radius.

For the speed-based definition, let \(C_*\) be the closed contour within the eddy with the largest circum-average geostrophic speed. Then

\[ L_s=\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal A(C_*)}{\pi}},\qquad U=\overline{|\mathbf v_g|}_{\,C_*}. \]

The paper favors this speed-based scale because it does not assume a Gaussian eddy shape. The explorer's area radius and pair distance must not be relabelled as \(L_s\), nor interpreted as independent measurements of maximum rotational speed.

5. Interpreting candidate diagnostics

The page records each candidate's first failed check. Later checks are marked Not evaluated, not failed. Runtime order is: domain boundary, missing data, pixel count, holes, interior extremum, amplitude, and pair distance. Threshold selection and four-neighbor component labeling occur before these checks.

Decision Interpretation
Touches the domain boundary A closed contour cannot be established beyond the cropped map.
Touches missing data The candidate or its immediate neighborhood meets an unavailable pixel; an unknown boundary is not treated as a valid SSH contour.
Size outside 8–999 pixels The connected component fails the paper's pixel-count limits.
Contains holes The selected region is not simply connected.
No qualifying interior local extremum No strict maximum/minimum has all four neighbors within the candidate.
Amplitude below 1 cm The measured extremum-to-perimeter-mean difference is too small.
Maximum pair distance reaches its limit The candidate fails the latitude-dependent compactness condition.
All implemented criteria passed The patch meets these snapshot conditions; persistence and nonlinearity remain untested.

The inspector shows the selected region, available qualifying extrema, pixel-value distributions, the perimeter mean, and measured values for the tests reached. A distribution can illustrate amplitude for an early rejection even when its amplitude criterion was not evaluated. The outcome table is the record of which checks actually ran.

On the overview, original SLA is the map background while boundaries are detected from the selected processed field. Diagnostic maps use the processed field. Full-scan maps emphasize accepted contours to avoid hundreds of overlapping rejected outlines; the complete candidate record remains in the filterable table and CSV export.

6. Tracking, lifetime selection, and nonlinearity

The Chelton Tracking Explorer links accepted detections on consecutive daily OSU fields. The procedure below describes this project's implementation: a regional daily adaptation with configurable search distances and measurement ratios.

6.1 Daily inputs and measurements

Each available day is processed independently with the selected background removal, followed by the full threshold scan: both polarities from −100 to +100 cm in 1 cm increments. Tracking always uses this scan, even though the detection explorer also offers a single-threshold experiment. A previous track does not influence the next day's detection.

Accepted regions are standardized before association. Tracking positions are spherical means of boundary vertices, rather than the detection page's mean pixel location or the paper's SSH-based centroid. Amplitude is the perimeter-mean amplitude from section 4.1. Tracking area is the spherical contour area, with area-equivalent radius \(r=\sqrt{S/\pi}\); these contour measurements can differ from the detection page's geographic pixel area.

Unavailable source dates are listed and supplied as empty detection days. An available day with no accepted regions is also empty for association. Coordinate-grid changes and processing failures stop the run with an error; they are not silently treated as missing observations.

6.2 Asymmetric search ellipse

For a previous center \((\phi_1,\lambda_1)\) and a candidate \((\phi_2,\lambda_2)\), let \(\Delta\lambda\) be the longitude difference wrapped to \([-\pi,\pi)\). With angles in radians and \(R=6371\) km, local offsets are

\[ x=R\cos\!\left(\frac{\phi_1+\phi_2}{2}\right)\Delta\lambda, \qquad y=R(\phi_2-\phi_1), \qquad \left(\frac{x}{a_x}\right)^2+ \left(\frac{y}{a_y}\right)^2\leq1. \]

The horizontal semi-axis \(a_x\) is the eastward limit when \(x\geq0\) and the westward limit otherwise; \(a_y\) is the north–south limit. Experimental defaults are 30 km east, 50 km west, and 30 km north–south. The ellipse is a hard eligibility gate. Eligible pairs are ranked by their great-circle distance, rather than the normalized ellipse value. A larger westward allowance is a geometric search preference, not a measured propagation speed.

6.3 Compatibility and greedy assignment

A candidate must have the same polarity as the track's last observation and pass both measurement-ratio gates:

\[ q_{A,\min}\leq\frac{A_{\mathrm{new}}}{A_{\mathrm{last}}}\leq q_{A,\max}, \qquad q_{S,\min}\leq\frac{S_{\mathrm{new}}}{S_{\mathrm{last}}}\leq q_{S,\max}. \]

Here \(A\) is amplitude and \(S\) is spherical contour area. Both intervals default to [0.25, 2.5], inclusive. Missing or nonpositive measurements cannot form a match. Ratios are relative to the last observation, not to the track's initial value or mean.

  1. Collect all eligible pairs between tracks observed yesterday and today's detections.
  2. Sort pairs by increasing great-circle distance. Exact ties use track ID, then today's input detection index.
  3. Accept a pair only when neither member has already been assigned. Each track and detection can be used once on this date.
  4. Close unmatched tracks. Every unassigned detection starts a new track with an incrementing run-local ID.

There is no missing-day recovery, motion prediction, splitting, or merging. A missing or empty day terminates every active track. For example, detections on days 1, 2, and 4 cannot form one continuous track: the day-4 detection starts a new one. Greedy nearest-pair assignment does not optimize all trajectories jointly.

6.4 Controls and lifetime filtering

Control Default Meaning
Duration 90 days 1–90 consecutive calendar days, including the starting date.
Background removal Custom Gaussian, σ = 4 cells The detection page's custom, paper-scale, or unfiltered choice is applied independently each day.
Search semi-axes 30 / 50 / 30 km East / west / north–south; all must be positive.
Amplitude and area ratios 0.25–2.5 each Positive lower and upper bounds, with the upper bound at least the lower bound.
Minimum retained lifetime 28 days Inclusive calendar span, at least one day and no greater than the selected duration.
\[L=(t_{\mathrm{last}}-t_{\mathrm{first}})_{\mathrm{days}}+1.\]

Lifetime filtering happens after association. Because this tracker never bridges gaps, lifetime equals the number of daily observations. Track IDs are not renumbered after filtering. Counts, polarity totals, mean and maximum lifetime, and mean radii describe retained tracks only; selecting a track changes the visualization, not the population statistics.

6.5 Interpreting trajectories

Map markers represent assigned daily centers and lines connect adjacent observations. The selected-track time series shows great-circle distance from the first assigned center, not cumulative travel distance or speed. Repeated coordinates record repeated detector locations; they do not prove that the physical eddy was stationary. The repeated-center summary excludes single-observation tracks from its comparable-track count.

Endpoints are limited by the selected interval and detection coverage: a retained track can begin before or continue after the displayed window. An interior endpoint can also reflect a failed detection or association. No trajectory smoothing or latitude-dependent Rossby propagation limit is applied. Assess parameter sensitivity before interpreting a sequence of matches as the identity of a physical eddy.

6.6 Paper comparison and nonlinearity

Appendix B.4 of the paper associates eddies across successive maps using a constrained search region and an SSH-based centroid. Its four-week retention and sixteen-week main-analysis population differ from this daily implementation. The default 28 inclusive days adapts four-week retention; the experimental daily search distances do not reproduce the paper's weekly sampling or its full catalogue.

The paper assesses nonlinearity using

\[\frac{U}{c}\gt1.\]

Here \(U\) is the maximum circum-average geostrophic speed and \(c\) is translation speed along the track. Neither the detection explorer nor this tracking implementation computes that nonlinearity metric. Passing the shape, amplitude, and association checks does not establish fluid trapping.

7. Differences from the paper and practical limitations

Aspect Preserved or implemented Important qualification
Identification rules Four-neighbor regions, simple connectivity, five criteria, strict interior extrema, and allowance for multiple extrema. Additional edge and missing-data guards are regional implementation decisions.
Full scan Both polarities, 1 cm levels, opposite scan directions, and retirement of accepted interior pixels. A single-threshold run is an exploratory extension, not the complete scan.
Filtering A Gaussian option is calibrated to the paper's 20° × 10° axial cutoff scales. The kernel is an approximation; the custom default has different cutoffs, and masks and map boundaries alter filter behavior.
Measurements Perimeter-mean amplitude, geographic pixel area, effective radius, and all-pairs great-circle compactness. The mean candidate latitude selects the distance limit; Gaussian eddy scales and the speed-based radius are not computed.
Data and sampling The method is applied to the supplied daily regional SLA fields. The paper uses a different global mapped product and seven-day sampling, with its own effective resolution.
Tracked population Single-map detection and a separate daily tracker with configurable lifetime retention are available. Section 6 describes the regional tracking adaptation. The paper's full catalogue, propagation-speed analysis, and nonlinearity classification are not reproduced.

Boundary and amplitude uncertainty

Appendix C emphasizes that an outermost acceptable SSH contour may exclude part of a physical eddy. The 1 cm contour increment alone can bias amplitude low by as much as about 1 cm, while boundary selection can also reduce inferred area and associated scales. That discretization statement is not a bound on total uncertainty: background flow, sampling, mapping, interactions, and the compactness restriction also matter.

Including more outer pixels can raise an estimated amplitude or radius while producing a less compact, less interpretable structure. Reducing a numerical threshold therefore does not automatically improve the result. The paper's boundary definition is operational, not a unique physical separation between an eddy and its surroundings.

Sensitivity and validation

For the evaluated 1993-01-01 dataset, the corrected full scan accepts 27 patches with the custom \(\sigma=4\) Gaussian, 26 with the paper-scale Gaussian approximation, and 25 without additional filtering. These are results from this regional day, not the paper's published catalogue or a ranking of detection accuracy.

Use the filter comparison and candidate diagnostics to identify which structures change and why. Independent labels and multiple dates are needed to assess detection accuracy. Geometric consistency and a high count alone do not validate an eddy population.

For a contrasting approach, the Nencioli methodology detects centers from four velocity-geometry constraints and estimates boundaries from a local streamfunction, rather than thresholding SSH regions.
