How Chelton tracking works

Each available daily SLA grid is optionally high-pass filtered, scanned by the existing Chelton detector, and standardized before the existing Chelton tracker is called. Centers are spherical means of contour vertices; radii are derived from spherical contour areas.

Candidates must have the same polarity, lie inside the configured east/west-asymmetric search ellipse, and satisfy both amplitude and area ratio intervals relative to the last observation. Eligible pairs across all active tracks are sorted by increasing great-circle distance and assigned greedily, with each track and detection used at most once per day. Exact-distance ties use track ID, then the detection index in that day's input list.

Only adjacent calendar days can match. Unmatched tracks close, and unmatched detections start new tracks. A missing or empty day breaks a trajectory. The minimum lifetime is applied after association; lifetime is the inclusive first-to-last calendar span and equals the observation count for these uninterrupted tracks.

Statistics describe all accepted tracks in the selected interval; selecting one only changes the visualization. Track IDs are run-local and may have gaps after lifetime filtering.

The 30 / 50 / 30 km east / west / north–south defaults are experimental daily OSU settings, not published search distances. The default 28-day retention adapts the paper's four-week minimum to the inclusive daily lifetime convention; it does not reproduce the paper's weekly sampling or stricter 16-week analysis population. No Rossby-speed-dependent western limit or trajectory smoothing is applied. Endpoint dates are limited by the chosen interval; geometric association does not independently validate eddy identity.
