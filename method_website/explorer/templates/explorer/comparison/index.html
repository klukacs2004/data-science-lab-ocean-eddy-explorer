Match same-day detections between methods

Each row proposes that two methods detected the same eddy on {{ run_settings.day|date:"Y-m-d" }}: for example, Chelton detection 12 and Halo detection 7. A and B are method-specific detections, not successive dates or track IDs. Click either detection to find its measurements below.

Compare standardized centers by great-circle distance and enclosed contours by intersection over union: IoU(A, B) = Area(A ∩ B) / Area(A ∪ B). An IoU of 0 means no area overlap; 1 means identical areas.

Detection A Detection B Center distance [km] IoU Polarities A / B Polarity check

Both thresholds are inclusive. Defaults (100 km, 0.1 IoU) are exploratory. Pairs must have known, agreeing polarities by default. “Include unresolved polarity” also permits pairs with at least one unknown value; these remain explicitly unresolved. Opposite known polarities never match.

Eligible pairs are sorted by decreasing IoU, then increasing center distance. Greedy assignment uses each detection once within each method pair. With “All method pairs”, one Chelton detection may therefore match both a Nencioli detection and a Halo detection in separate rows; this does not establish a consistent three-method group. Invalid contours have unavailable IoU and are excluded. Areas use spherical great-circle contour edges. These are geometric proposals, not independently verified eddy identities or temporal tracks. Adjusting thresholds does not rerun detection.

What is being compared?

Definitions and scope

All methods load the same daily SLA and geostrophic velocity grid. Chelton scans both polarities; Nencioli detects velocity centers and estimates boundaries; this project's Okubo–Weiss method is the Halo hybrid of negative W and closed height contours.

Standardization uses the same contour-based area and area-equivalent radius for every method. Map locations are spherical means of boundary vertices, rather than native dynamical centers. Nencioli's circular fallback, when accepted, remains an estimated boundary.

Chelton polarity comes from SLA maxima/minima; Nencioli uses velocity rotation and hemisphere. For this daily comparison, Halo polarity comes from the area-weighted mean signed relative vorticity strictly inside its contour: ζ = ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y. In the Northern Hemisphere positive means cyclonic and negative anticyclonic; the classification reverses in the Southern Hemisphere. W alone has no rotation sign. Missing interior samples, a zero or nonfinite mean, or an equator-touching or crossing Halo contour retain unknown polarity; the evidence column explains why. A nonzero mean is a classification, not a calibrated confidence estimate.

Only Chelton supplies native amplitude, so amplitudes are not summarized across methods. Halo's use here with SLA is an adaptation of the paper's absolute-SSH method. The detections CSV includes polarity evidence and Halo mean relative vorticity in s⁻¹.

Optional Chelton background removal changes its detection field; the combined map still displays the original SLA. Pairwise geometric matching compares snapshot detections. Daily mode performs no tracking or lifetime filtering. Tracking mode reuses the existing method trackers over the chosen period. These comparisons are not validation against independent labels.
