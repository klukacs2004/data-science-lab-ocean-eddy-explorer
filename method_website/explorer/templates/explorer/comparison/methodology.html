1. Comparison scope and inputs

The Comparison Explorer applies Chelton, Nencioli, and Halo to the daily OSU blended altimetry dataset over the U.S. West Coast, on its 0.25° latitude–longitude grid. Daily detection compares one date; tracking compares the same consecutive calendar period for all three methods.

The source variables are sea-level anomaly (SLA) in metres and eastward and northward geostrophic velocity in metres per second. The daily comparison requires their coordinates to align exactly. The map displays the original SLA in centimetres, including when Chelton uses optional background removal.

Shared detection means a common source grid and a common description of accepted features. Shared tracking additionally applies the same temporal association rules to each method's detections. These control different parts of the experiment: detection determines which daily features exist; tracking determines how observations are linked.

The separate shared tracker is described in section 4. The current Comparison page's Tracking mode runs each method's own tracker, as detailed in section 5. Its distributions therefore reflect differences in both detection and temporal association.

2. Shared detection

2.1 Independent detectors on one grid

All three methods analyze the selected source day independently. Their acceptance tests remain method-specific; the comparison does not impose a common boundary or require a feature to be accepted by more than one detector.

2.2 Common centers, areas, and radii

Accepted features are represented by a closed geographic contour, a method-local ID, a date, a center, polarity, area, and radius. Fractional grid-index boundaries are interpolated onto latitude and longitude; longitudes are normalized to [−180°, 180°).

The standardized center is the spherical mean of the contour's distinct boundary vertices. Nencioli's original velocity-based center is retained separately. Every method uses the same spherical polygon area S, with great-circle edges on a sphere of radius 6371 km, and the same area-equivalent radius:

\[ R_{\mathrm{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{S}{\pi}}. \]

This makes the reported geometry comparable in km² and km. It does not make the underlying boundaries equivalent. Halo's maximum loop diameter is a detection gate and differs from twice this radius. Native amplitude is currently available only for Chelton; missing amplitudes remain unavailable.

2.3 Polarity and boundary reliability

Chelton SLA maxima are labeled anticyclonic and minima cyclonic for this Northern Hemisphere study. Nencioli uses the detected velocity rotation and hemisphere. The daily comparison classifies Halo from signed, area-weighted contour-mean relative vorticity and the hemisphere; negative W alone carries no rotation sign. Missing, zero, or otherwise unresolved Halo means, and contours touching or crossing the equator, retain unknown polarity.

Nencioli distinguishes streamfunction boundaries from circular fallback geometry. A fallback or unknown boundary source supplies no reliable physical radius. The daily table and basic statistics report the standardized geometry of all accepted detections; the tracking radius distributions and radius-evolution curves include only reliable, finite, positive radii. Fallback observations can still contribute to track counts and observed spans.

3. Matching detections between methods

Same-day matching proposes correspondences between two detectors' accepted features. It uses great-circle distance between standardized centers and the intersection over union of their spherical contour areas:

\[ \operatorname{IoU}(A,B)= \frac{\operatorname{Area}(A\cap B)} {\operatorname{Area}(A\cup B)}. \]

The default inclusive gates are center distance ≤ 100 km and IoU ≥ 0.1, with known, agreeing polarities. The unresolved-polarity option also permits pairs with at least one unknown value. Opposite known polarities are always excluded. Invalid contours have unavailable IoU and cannot match.

Eligible pairs are sorted by decreasing IoU, then increasing center distance. Greedy assignment uses each detection once within each method pair. A Chelton detection can therefore appear once against Nencioli and once against Halo when all pairs are displayed. This pairwise selection does not establish a consistent three-method group.

The thresholds are exploratory and update the proposed matches without rerunning detection. Detection IDs are local to their method and date; equal IDs carry no identity information. Same-day matching and temporal tracking answer separate questions.

4. Shared tracking

4.1 Common association rules

The shared tracker provides a controlled temporal-association experiment: freeze each method's daily detection catalogue, then apply the same polarity, displacement, area-consistency, and assignment rules to each catalogue separately. A trajectory contains detections from one method throughout. The daily detections and their boundaries remain unchanged.

The primary center policy uses standardized contour centers for every method. A separate native-preferred sensitivity can use Nencioli's original velocity center when available. Halo needs signed vorticity-based polarity annotations: unknown polarity never establishes a temporal identity in this tracker.

4.2 Eligibility and matching score

For an observation on day t and a candidate on day t + 1, require known, equal polarity and great-circle displacement \(d\leq d_{\max}\). With area consistency enabled, both observations must have reliable, positive, finite areas. Their ratio \(r=S_{t+1}/S_t\) must satisfy the symmetric gate

\[ \frac{1}{F}\leq r\leq F, \qquad F=2\text{ by default}. \]

The displacement limit is an explicit experiment parameter. Eligible pairs receive the dimensionless score

\[ J=\sqrt{ \left(\frac{d}{s_x}\right)^2+ \left(\frac{\log r}{\log F_s}\right)^2 }. \]

By default, \(s_x=d_{\max}\) and \(F_s=F\). The two score scales can be fixed independently of the eligibility gates so that a gate sensitivity leaves assignment preferences comparable. Removing the area-ratio gate can retain the area term; the displacement-only ablation removes both the area gate and area score, while preserving known equal polarity.

The shared score uses no detector-specific amplitude or vorticity-magnitude term. Nencioli fallback and unknown boundary areas cannot enter area-consistent matching.

4.3 Assignment and track endings

On each date, global one-to-one assignment first maximizes the number of eligible links, then minimizes their total score. A detection can extend at most one track, and a track can acquire at most one observation per date. Unassigned detections start new tracks; unmatched tracks end.

Only adjacent calendar days are linked. An empty or unavailable day breaks continuity. There is no gap recovery, future look-ahead, trajectory interpolation, splitting, or merging. All resulting segments, including single-day tracks, are retained. Link and daily diagnostics support auditing of rejected candidates and assignment conflicts.

These common rules help isolate the effect of the detector on observed spans. They are a separate experimental tracking procedure; the current Comparison interface uses the method-specific association rules below.

5. Tracking used by the Comparison page

Choose Tracking to run the three existing trackers over a shared start date and duration. The default period is 90 days; the comparison accepts 1–365 consecutive days and requires an available source grid for every date. The selected detection settings apply on every day. The comparison retains all track segments from one day, overriding the standalone explorers' minimum-span filters.

Halo excludes detections without a usable signed contour-mean vorticity before tracking. Its current tracking catalogue can retain unknown standardized polarity because association uses the separate signed-vorticity statistic. This differs from the daily comparison's polarity annotation and the shared tracker's known-polarity requirement.

The results list the association settings used by each tracker. A fully available input period can still contain days with no accepted detections; each tracker handles those according to its gap rules. Observed span includes both endpoint dates:

\[ T=(t_{\mathrm{last}}-t_{\mathrm{first}})_{\mathrm{days}}+1. \]

A four-day span with a recovered missing day has three observations. A track touching the first or last day of the selected period is boundary-censored and may continue beyond the observation window.

6. Interpreting the tracking figures

6.1 Figure 7: size and observed-span distributions

The upper panel bins reliable daily radii in 2 km intervals. Each method is normalized by its own total number of reliable, finite, positive radius observations. Longer tracks contribute more observations, so this is an observation-weighted size distribution.

The lower panel is the empirical upper-tail distribution of observed spans. Every track contributes once, including single-day and boundary-censored tracks:

\[ P(T\geq d)=100\, \frac{\#\{\text{tracks with }T\geq d\}} {\#\{\text{all tracks}\}}. \]

The percentage axis is logarithmic. The span axis fits the curves above the 0.3% display floor; a display limit does not remove tracks from the denominator. This empirical curve does not correct for censoring and is not an estimate of complete physical lifetimes.

6.2 Figure 8: radius evolution

Tracks are grouped by observed span into [7, 25), [25, 50), [50, 100), [100, 150), [150, 200), and [200, 250] days. Shared endpoints belong to the next class, except that 250 days is included in the last class. Tracks outside these classes remain in Figure 7.

Within a class with midpoint \(T_c\), each observation's elapsed calendar age \(a=(t-t_{\mathrm{first}})_{\mathrm{days}}\) is scaled by its track's observed span:

\[ x=\frac{a}{T}\,T_c, \qquad b=\lfloor x\rfloor. \]

Observations are collected into one-day bins on this class-scaled axis and drawn at bin centers. First average reliable radii within each track and bin, then average those track means with equal weight. Radius stays in absolute kilometres; it is not divided by a track's mean or maximum radius. Missing observations are not interpolated.

By default, a visible bin needs at least 20 contributing tracks. Bins below the selected minimum are left as gaps. Tracks touching either period boundary are excluded from Figure 8 by default; the inclusion control adds them back without changing Figure 7. An absent curve can reflect insufficient contributors or an empty span class.

7. Controls and outputs

Start with the same daily input and the default detection settings, inspect differences in accepted boundaries, then vary one setting at a time. Daily results include the combined map, geometric matching, counts and size statistics, and the standardized detection table.

The daily exports provide all detections, full-run basic statistics, and the currently selected matching pairs as separate CSV files; the combined map is downloadable as an image. Detection-table filters and map visibility do not recompute the full-run statistics. The detection CSV includes boundary provenance, radius reliability, polarity evidence, and native Nencioli centers where available.

Tracking runs in the background, with progress and a saved run link that can be bookmarked and reopened. Completed daily detections are reused where available. Changing the minimum contributors per age bin or the boundary-track inclusion setting reuses the completed tracking run. Plot toolbars support image export.

8. Interpretation and limitations

A larger detection count, a higher contour overlap, or longer observed tracks does not by itself establish a more accurate method. These regional SLA implementations and their exploratory association settings have different sensitivity to resolution, missing data, preprocessing, and boundary construction.

Common area and radius definitions harmonize reported geometry; shared temporal rules additionally control association. The current Comparison Tracking mode preserves method-specific association, so its span differences cannot be attributed to detection alone.

Window boundaries, failed daily detections, and failed associations can truncate tracks. Even tracks contained within the selected period have observed spans rather than proven physical birth-to-decay lifetimes. Inspect contributor counts and parameter sensitivity before interpreting radius growth or differences between methods.

For each detector's acceptance criteria and reconstruction choices, see the Chelton, Nencioli, and Halo methodology pages.
